English rendering of the channel post (Korean preview was the source text):

JPM ServiceNow core partner feedback. The note summarizes a channel check with 10 ServiceNow partners and customers, including third-party checks. Most partners said ServiceNow-related business is still growing at double digits; some above 20%. The spread depends on the customer mix. Large-enterprise partners reported about 24% year-over-year growth in 2026 Q3. IT solution firms reported 20–25%. A global hybrid-cloud executive reported growth a bit above 5%. BFSI partners reported flat to a 5% decline — weaker where budgets are tight.

Some partners said deal cycles are longer because of macro pressure, tariff concerns, tighter CFO review, and questions about AI ROI. Manufacturing sales cycles moved from 3–4 months to 6–9 months in cited cases; others said cycles lengthened 10–15%. The delays sit in large, complex deals. The overall read was delay, not disappearance of demand.

Renewals were the clearest strength. Partners cited a 100% renewal rate or no downgrades, and said ServiceNow is embedded across ITOM, HR, and SecOps so replacement is hard. One expert said that when reps oversold, unused licenses shrink about 30% at renewal — utilization, not churn.

Deal size is generally 10–20% larger as customers add modules and consolidate software. Pricing ranged from flat to double-digit increases. One senior enterprise sales executive said SKU prices rose more than 15% with full AI-feature bundling on 2026-07-01; other large partners cited 12–15%. A shift toward consumption and token pricing is both an opportunity and a budget-predictability concern, but it was not described as changing the intent to stay on the platform. Advanced is the preferred tier; one ITSM senior director said 100% of the active pipeline is Advanced and Prime is zero.

Executive interest in AI is nearly universal, but production use and ROI are uneven. Unpredictable token cost, data quality, missing governance, and change management were named as barriers. In ServiceNow’s AI positioning, Moveworks (recently rebranded Otto) and AI Control Tower are in front; Now Assist is secondary in marketing. One partner said Now Assist needed heavy engineering while Moveworks showed results faster. AI Control Tower is framed as one pane to discover, govern, secure, observe, and measure enterprise AI workflows — an orchestration layer that complements, rather than replaces, detection tools such as Palo Alto Networks or CrowdStrike.

On acquisitions, Moveworks was said to show near-term value especially in HRSD automation, while Armis and Veza were described as specialized security-adjacent assets whose importance depends on platform integration over the next 12 months. (Those names are not separate entity cards.)

Competition: ServiceNow was described as still effectively unchallenged in complex enterprise ITSM. Jira competes in cost-sensitive or narrow ITSM evaluations. Microsoft and large-suite vendors were treated as bundle or adjacency risk more than a direct substitute, with Microsoft called a longer-term threat. Partners said platform breadth, cross-department integration, and the installed base keep the moat wide. Platform consolidation across IT, HR, security, and compliance, on top of ITOM and CMDB demand, was named the strongest growth driver.
