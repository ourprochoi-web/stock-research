Saudi Arabia has unexpectedly cut November crude oil prices for Asia, while raising those for north-west Europe and the Mediterranean regions, a pricing document showed on Oct 5. The largest Middle Eastern crude exporter set the November Arab Light crude oil official selling price (OSP) to Asia at US$5 a barrel, below the average of Oman and Dubai prices, down US$3 from the previous month. The discount for November is the widest since June 2020, Reuters data showed. This was against expectations of a US$3 a barrel hike for its November OSP in a Reuters survey in line with gains seen in the Middle Eastern benchmarks. State oil company Saudi Aramco made deeper cuts of US$5 a barrel for the November OSPs of heavier grades – Arab Medium and Arab Heavy – sold to Asia. Aramco has been looking at offering discounts for oil loaded off Oman to compensate buyers for record freight rates, sources familiar with the matter said last week, as it sought to protect its market share after the regional conflict hit exports. Separately, Saudi Aramco raised the November OSPs for north-west Europe by US$3 a barrel across all grades after it resumed exports from the Red Sea port of Yanbu. Aramco kept prices unchanged for buyers in the United States. Saudi Arabia and other members of OPEC+ agreed on Oct 4 to keep oil production targets steady for November, in line with expectations that further output policy adjustments are unlikely until 2027.
