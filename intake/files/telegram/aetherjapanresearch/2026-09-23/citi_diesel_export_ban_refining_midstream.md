## Facts to preserve

### Citi US refining — export-ban support rising (25453)

- Diesel retail prices are above **$6.5/gal**. Citi attributes the pressure to the Iran conflict, Russia's temporary diesel export ban, and Ukraine strikes on Russian refineries: Syzran (9/15), Yaroslavl (9/17), Moscow (9/20), and Novoil (9/21).
- Rep. Tim Burchett has two early-stage bills: (1) a full diesel export ban through January 2027; and (2) controls if the national average exceeds **$5/gal**, released below **$4.50/gal**. Chuck Grassley supports a ban, John Thune is open to considering one, and Doug Burgum is publicly opposed.
- If a full ban blocks about **1.3 mbd** of net exports, Citi estimates distillate inventory rises from about **108 mb** to about **180 mb** in roughly **two months**. That is the July 2020 peak / post-2001 high; the all-time high was about **186 mb** in 1982.
- 2020 context: refinery utilization fell from the 90s to the high 60s; at the inventory peak it was in the mid-to-high 70s, Gulf Coast ULSD was about **$1.2/gal**, and cracks were negative.
- Price effects would be regional. **PADD 1 is a net distillate importer.** US diesel exports go to LatAm (**>700k b/d**), Europe (**~300k b/d**), Africa (**~150k b/d**), with the remainder to Asia, North America, and the Caribbean.
- Refiners would likely cut runs as margins fall, creating gasoline and jet-fuel spillover. Gulf Coast refiners with high middle-distillate yields are most exposed. 2Q26 middle-distillate yields: **VLO ~39%, MPC ~38%, PSX ~40%, PBF 36%**. PBF has the lower yield but, according to Citi, its cost structure is most hurt by a crack-spread decline.
- A tariff would raise prices in Europe and LatAm, where dependence on US refined products is high. A soft quota would have limited impact; a hard quota would raise domestic inventories and weaken margins. Citi says a full ban would likely end the historical refining-margin / refiner-stock rally.

### Citi North America midstream — product export-ban impact (25454)

- Citi's four buckets are: direct exports (**mild negative**), inland pipelines/storage (**positive**), fuel distribution (**positive**), and foreign refining (**positive**). The headline effect is larger than the operating effect; the ban would likely be short-lived, and early discussion covers diesel, which is **under 50% of US product exports**.
- EPD's EBITDA hit is estimated below **1%**. KMI liquids terminals are majority take-or-pay, making a **1–2 quarter** ban digestible. If the Jones Act waiver through mid-November is extended, foreign ships can move Gulf Coast product to US import terminals.
- Inland storage and volatility are offsets for **SUN, ET, MPLX, and OKE**; OKE has the largest pipeline network. Fuel distribution makes SUN the largest beneficiary: each **$0.01/gal** increase in US fuel margin is about **$120M+** of annual EBITDA, roughly **3% of 2026e**. A diesel-only ban weakens this effect.
- SUN's Burnaby refinery in Canada is outside the ban; Citi estimates refining EBITDA could exceed **$700M/year**, more than twice the past level. Butane blending is a risk if gasoline yield is maximized; OKE is exposed, though 2026 is hedged. Run cuts would pressure blending and refinery-bound volumes, with MPLX exposed and MPC MVC providing protection.
- Citi's conclusion: **SUN** is the maximum beneficiary; **ET** benefits from volatility / regional arbitrage; EPD and OKE export-dock headwinds can be more than offset by inland exposure; **KMI and MPLX** are the most defensive. Ratings at 9/21 close: Buy **ET $20.9, EPD $38.47, OKE $91.8, SUNC $77.52**; Neutral **KMI $31.72, MPLX $59.09**.
