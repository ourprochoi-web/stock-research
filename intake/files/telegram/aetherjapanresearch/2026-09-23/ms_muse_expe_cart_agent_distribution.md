## Facts to preserve

### Expedia connector and the OTA prisoner's dilemma

- Expedia is the **first OTA** to partner with Muse. Morgan Stanley expects a connector that gives Muse access to hotel, air, and package search and booking; this is **not Muse browsing the Expedia website**.
- The integration details and timing remain open. Key questions are **merchant of record**, **unit economics**, **data ownership**, **inventory scope**, and **loyalty linkage**.
- Expedia's first-mover position raises pressure on Booking Holdings and Airbnb. In the near term, Expedia can obtain incremental rooms at low customer-acquisition cost. If planning and booking move to general agents and Expedia SKUs are shown more often or by default, rivals may need similar partnerships to avoid share loss.
- Morgan Stanley compares the dynamic with the **Google paid-search** prisoner's dilemma: suppliers may have to participate in the distribution layer even when the pressure is not inherently positive.
- Booking Holdings' execution question is whether differentiated supply, reviews, and room-level data can convert agent traffic into direct and repeat customers.
- Airbnb has roughly **90% direct traffic** and marketing below **2% of bookings**, versus **4–5%** for Booking Holdings and Expedia. It therefore has more to lose if discovery and booking migrate to general agents and it must bear higher acquisition costs.

### Meta: inspiration and mid-funnel into transactions

- The partnership moves Meta beyond Reels/Stories travel inspiration and the mid-funnel toward transactions.
- Offline travel is roughly **$1T**, about **3% of roughly $30T** of offline spend. Booking Holdings, Expedia, and Airbnb performance-ad spend is estimated at roughly **$15B this year**.
- Morgan Stanley frames the Expedia deal as **Scenario 3: formal agent distribution**. Near term, the unit economics could be better than advertising for incremental volume; long term, there is a risk that traffic costs become permanent and rise as agents own the top of funnel.

### Instacart: retail inventory access versus intermediation

- Instacart also partnered with Muse. A user can turn a prompt such as “Taco Tuesday” into a cart at a preferred retailer and complete checkout and delivery.
- The capability is similar to Instacart's Clementine AI shopping assistant. Instacart is described as the first major third-party delivery provider to integrate with general LLMs; this is its **fourth general integration**.
- Muse can intermediate between consumers and Instacart, but the partnership also provides access to inventory across thousands of retailers and to picking, packing, and delivery. Online grocery penetration remains low, with roughly **$1.6T** of grocery spend still offline.
- At roughly **6× 2028 EBITDA**, amid long-term encroachment by Walmart and Amazon, the partnership can improve Instacart's competitive position and funnel opportunity.
