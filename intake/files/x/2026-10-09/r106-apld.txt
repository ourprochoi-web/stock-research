$APLD CEO calls its data centers a franchise. In practice it's one 150 MW building, designed once and copied.

How the franchise works:
1. One standard building: six data halls, two 75 MW power systems, the same floor plan everywhere
2. The same suppliers on every site, bought in bulk across a 1 GW+ pipeline
3. The same 15-20 key roles at each campus, so a new site doesn't need a new team
4. Built in 14-18 months, then leased for 15 years on take-or-pay terms

What one building is worth, on the $APLD OWN numbers:
1. Costs about $1.8B to build
2. Earns about $218M a year in NOI
3. Pays for itself in roughly 8 years of a 15-year lease

Where the franchise is already showing up:
1. Speed. Polaris Forge 1 phases have landed on schedule
2. Margin. Q1 came in near 89%, above the guide
3. Price. Expansions on existing campuses are pricing about 15% higher

Where it isn't yet: cost per MW. Every campus still carries the same $11-$13 M band, and $WFC Wells Fargo models the pipeline higher, at $13-$13.5 M. A true franchise should get cheaper with each copy. So far this one gets faster and earns more instead.

For scaling this is perfect. The October plan of about 1 GW a year after 2028 means roughly 6 or 7 of these buildings a year, and about $12B of construction annually.

The design is repeatable. The financing is what decides how fast it gets copied.

Long $APLD.
