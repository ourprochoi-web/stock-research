$LITE 

Lumentum (LITE) said revenue for the quarter that ended in September should come in around $1,250M, up 134% from a year earlier. On October 9, reports quoted the CEO as saying its output is effectively sold out through early 2029. Yet the stock then fell 24.4% over ten days, closing at $834.51 on October 19. This piece uses the numbers people use to value optical component makers to explain the gap.

1. What the company does [Fig. 1]
- $LITE makes laser chips for optical communications on InP, a compound semiconductor wafer. These lasers are where a data center's electrical signals become light, and the company also assembles them into modules that send and receive data as light (optical transceivers).
- Its fiscal year ends in late June. Revenue in the fourth quarter of last fiscal year (FY26) more than doubled from a year earlier, and the company pointed to demand for optical links in AI data centers.
- The next growth area is links between GPUs inside the server rack. If that stretch, run on copper today, moves to light, far more high-power lasers are needed. According to call coverage, the company said high-volume shipments for this use start in the second half of 2027.

2. Capacity, not demand, sets the ceiling on revenue [Fig. 1, 3]
- According to reports citing an October 9 Bloomberg interview, the CEO said $LITE is effectively sold out through early 2029. He said it cannot meet about 70% of demand for some products through 2027, and about 30% for others through 2028.
- Being sold out means demand is strong, but it also means revenue cannot grow faster than the factories can expand. Per the coverage, the CEO said new laser lines take years to reach customers.
- The company's September-quarter revenue outlook was $1,225–1,275M. Analysts expect $1,470M for the October–December quarter, 17.6% above that midpoint.

3. Higher prices are part of why margins rose [Fig. 1]
- Gross margin on the company's own measure, which leaves out stock paid to employees and acquisition-related amortization, was 50.4% in the fourth quarter: about $50 of every $100 in sales, up from about $38 a year earlier.
- According to call coverage, the company cited factory utilization, product mix and price increases on some products. It could raise prices while supply was short; when supply loosens, that part could shrink.

4. On results day only $LITE rose; last week the whole optical group fell [Fig. 3]
- The day after its August 11 report, $LITE rose 13.6%. $SMH rose only 2.1% that day. It was $LITE's own results that moved the stock.
- The 24.4% drop from October 9 to 19 came mostly from the sector. Over the same span $SMH fell 9.1%, and three optical component and equipment makers ($COHR, $CIEN, $AAOI) fell 26.0% on average. There was no new company filing; coverage at the time pointed to money moving into defensive stocks.
- Compared with the close on results day, $LITE is 1.7% higher while $SMH is 4.3% lower. The three optical companies fell 27.5% on average, and $QQQ was roughly flat. $LITE fell less while its sector, not the market, dropped; that gap is the piece of today's price that belongs to $LITE alone, and it shows up next as a multiple premium.

5. 25% pricier than comparison companies on next year's earnings, similar on the year after [Fig. 2]
- Earnings here are analysts' per-share estimates on the company's own measure. On this fiscal year's (FY27, ending June 2027) expected $21.79 per share, $LITE's P/E is 38.3x. That is 25% above the 30.7x average for five optical-related comparison companies ($COHR, $AAOI, $CIEN, $MRVL, $FN).
- On next fiscal year's (FY28) expected $36.02, it is 23.2x, similar to the same five companies' 22.3x average on the year after next.
- Working back at the 30.7x comparison average, today's price needs earnings of about $27.2 per share, between the FY27 and FY28 estimates. Seen this way, the market has already priced in much of the large earnings increase expected for FY28. This is a calculation, not a price target.
- Analysts' FY28 estimates themselves range from $30.38 to $40.90 per share, and comparison multiples have come down sharply since August. If either moves, the result of the same calculation changes.

6. The risks that remain: customer concentration and share count [Fig. 2]
- One unnamed customer was 26.6% of last fiscal year's revenue, up from 15.4% a year earlier. If one or two customers change order timing, a quarter can swing a lot.
- Common shares rose 27% in a year because part of the convertible bonds was exchanged for stock. The large fourth-quarter net loss under official accounting came from a book loss on that exchange, which the company called one-time and non-cash. Still, with more shares, per-share earnings grow more slowly than total earnings.

7. What investors should watch now (Q1 FY27 results, Nov 5) [Fig. 3]
- Whether the October–December revenue outlook beats the $1,470M analyst estimate. For a company whose revenue is set by capacity, beating it would be evidence that expansion is turning into sales, and would support today's multiple premium.
- Whether gross margin on the company's measure holds near 50%. It shows whether price increases from the shortage are sticking.
- Whether the company confirms on the call the "sold out through early 2029" remark and its expansion plans, which so far come only from news reports. Confirmation would give more reason to trust the FY28 estimates.
- The 26.6% customer concentration, and moves more than 2x SMH's when the sector swings, remain as risks.
