SpaceX $SPCX just put out its second report as a public company, and the center of gravity has moved from rockets to compute.

The beat was not bought with an acquisition. Take out the $608 million Cursor added after the August 14 close and revenue is still about $14.0 billion, well above where the Street sat. Cloud services contracts inside SI did the heavy lifting.

Key catalysts from the report:
1. Starship flew its first paying mission, 26 Starlink V3 satellites with roughly 10x the downlink of a Falcon 9 load
2. A deal for 800 MHz spectrum to build out Starlink Mobile, expected to close in the first half of 2027
3. More cloud services agreements signed in the quarter, plus the launch of Grok Bot
4. New launch backlog: three more NASA crew missions, 18 national security launches and a lunar payload on Starship

Bret Johnsen $SPCX, Chief Financial Officer:
'𝙏𝙝𝙚 𝙘𝙤𝙢𝙗𝙞𝙣𝙖𝙩𝙞𝙤𝙣 𝙤𝙛 𝙤𝙪𝙧 𝙡𝙖𝙪𝙣𝙘𝙝 𝙡𝙚𝙖𝙙𝙚𝙧𝙨𝙝𝙞𝙥, 𝙧𝙚𝙘𝙪𝙧𝙧𝙞𝙣𝙜 𝙎𝙩𝙖𝙧𝙡𝙞𝙣𝙠 𝙧𝙚𝙫𝙚𝙣𝙪𝙚, 𝙖𝙣𝙙 𝙩𝙝𝙚 𝙨𝙞𝙜𝙣𝙞𝙛𝙞𝙘𝙖𝙣𝙩 𝙚𝙭𝙥𝙖𝙣𝙨𝙞𝙤𝙣 𝙤𝙛 𝙤𝙪𝙧 𝙎𝙄 𝙞𝙣𝙛𝙧𝙖𝙨𝙩𝙧𝙪𝙘𝙩𝙪𝙧𝙚 𝙘𝙤𝙣𝙩𝙞𝙣𝙪𝙚𝙨 𝙩𝙤 𝙙𝙧𝙞𝙫𝙚 𝙢𝙚𝙖𝙣𝙞𝙣𝙜𝙛𝙪𝙡 𝙤𝙥𝙚𝙧𝙖𝙩𝙞𝙣𝙜 𝙡𝙚𝙫𝙚𝙧𝙖𝙜𝙚.'

Starlink is the steady part. Enterprise and government contracts are growing faster than consumer plans, and ARPU ticked up from last quarter after falling over the past year. SI swung to an operating profit from a loss one quarter ago while taking most of the spending. Space was the soft spot, with fewer launches and heavier Starship R&D.

Net income got help below the operating line from other income, more than half of it from a related party, plus a tax benefit. Cursor also comes with $23.4 billion of stock awards tied to future service, and that cost runs through the income statement as it vests.

Inflection point: SI is now the largest revenue line at SpaceX $SPCX, ahead of Starlink, at a similar EBITDA margin.

At roughly a $2 trillion market value, I want to see the SI capex turn into free cash flow before I treat this as more than a stock to track.

SpaceX $SPCX said a lot more on the call than in the release:

1. Q1 2027 revenue guided to $33B to $37B. The Street was near $22.2B. That is more than double Q3.

2. Q4 capex guided to $35B to $40B, nearly double Q3.

3. Backlog of $63B, with about $42B expected to be recognized within a year.

4. Compute is renting slightly above the top of the $30 to $50 per watt range given last quarter. Payback is under a year, and contracts are renewing at higher prices.

5. $SPCX keeps compute on short-term contracts at spot rates and splits it roughly 50/50 between its own use and renting out.

6. Grok Bot reached 800,000 paid weekly users, more than doubling in a month.

7. A 440,000 Vera Rubin training cluster is targeted by the end of Q2 2027.

8. SpaceX casts its own turbine blades and vanes. Musk says that brings power online 12 to 18 months faster than rivals.

9. Starship weekly in H1 2027, twice weekly by H2. The Flight 15 license is expected to include tower catches of both stages.

10. Starlink targets about 100x today's bandwidth within roughly three years, on around 100,000 satellites. Musk sees a path to several hundred billion dollars a year in connectivity revenue.

11. Roughly 12,000 aircraft are contracted for Starlink, almost a quarter of the world's fleet.

12. Starlink Mobile has 22M monthly users, +37% QoQ. The FCC approved 15,000 Mobile V2 satellites, and US service is targeted for early 2028.

13. Terafab is aiming for a first test chip around May or June 2027 and volume production around Q3 2028.

14. Both SpaceX and Tesla $TSLA have special committees on a merger. No agreement on terms, and Tesla would need a majority of all shares.

Elon Musk $SPCX, Chief Executive Officer:
'𝙎𝙤 𝙬𝙚 𝙘𝙤𝙪𝙡𝙙 𝙧𝙚𝙣𝙩 𝙤𝙪𝙩 𝙤𝙪𝙧 𝙘𝙤𝙢𝙥𝙪𝙩𝙚 𝙨𝙚𝙫𝙚𝙧𝙖𝙡 𝙩𝙞𝙢𝙚𝙨 𝙤𝙫𝙚𝙧 𝙞𝙛 𝙬𝙚 𝙨𝙖𝙞𝙙 𝙮𝙚𝙨 𝙩𝙤 𝙚𝙫𝙚𝙧𝙮𝙤𝙣𝙚.'
