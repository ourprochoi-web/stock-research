Samik Chatterjee's note today calls the cash burn a timing issue. I agree, and the timing still has to be financed at today's prices.

A new GW costs close to four years of what a running GW earns. Most of each build is borrowed against its own contract. The parent covers the rest from cash, bonds, converts or new shares. The dollar 2032 notes priced at par in June with a 9.625% coupon. This week they yielded more than 15%.

J.P. Morgan pushes back on Gartner's neocloud math on three counts:
1. Compute demand has sped up in recent months
2. $CRWV sells capacity before it builds, on take-or-pay contracts
3. Most of the spend is GPUs and other short-lived gear, so orders can stop fast if demand fades

Take-or-pay moves the AI revenue risk onto the buyers. If that revenue comes in short, I'd expect the first test to be a large customer asking to renegotiate or push out delivery.

Banks usually underwrite a GPU over 3 to 4 years, Impax portfolio manager Tony Trzcinka told Reuters this month. The contracts behind $CRWV's August GPU loan sit inside that window, so the return on that book rides on what the chips earn afterward.

The next GPU loan will show whether terms are improving: a spread inside SOFR plus 5.50%, or contracts longer than 3 years.

Year-end active power above the 1.85 GW guide would show the build is on schedule.
